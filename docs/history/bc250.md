# What is the BC-250?

The BC-250 is a compact computer board built around an AMD processor with Radeon graphics. It can run Linux and
games like a desktop PC. This suite provides tools made specifically for its hardware and for Bazzite.

## Where it comes from

AMD made the BC-250 for cryptocurrency mining. ASRock Rack built it into a mining system, the ASRock 4U12G BC-250.
It is a 4U rack server with twelve BC-250 boards side by side. It appeared in early 2022, when mining Ethereum with
graphics chips still paid. The complete rack with its twelve boards was listed at 13,499 euros. That was about
15,000 US dollars at the time. Its reported speed was 610 MH/s, about 50 MH/s per board. At that speed, mining blogs
expected it to pay itself back in roughly 440 to 530 days.

The processor on the board is a cut-down relative of the PlayStation 5's chip. Its code name is *Ariel*. It has six
Zen 2 CPU cores and an RDNA 2 graphics part. That graphics part has 24 of its compute units switched on, where the
PlayStation 5 uses 36. Reports at the time suggested the boards use console chips that did not pass Sony's tests,
with parts disabled. AMD never confirmed this.

People sometimes call it a Bitcoin-mining board, but it isn't a dedicated Bitcoin-mining chip. It combines a
general-purpose CPU with a GPU. Miners used GPUs for other cryptocurrencies, such as Ethereum. Special-purpose
hardware dominates Bitcoin mining.

## How it became a standalone board

In September 2022 Ethereum stopped mining altogether with its switch to proof of stake. The mining rigs had nothing
left to earn. Their owners took them apart and sold the boards on the second-hand market. The boards came with no
case, no power supply and no cooling of their own. They sold for a fraction of what they cost new. That caught the
attention of PC enthusiasts, who saw an unusual, low-cost platform for Linux gaming and hardware experiments. Every
board on sale today comes from a dismantled mining rig. None are made new.

The start was not promising. In March 2024 the YouTube channel Craft Computing bought a brand-new rack with its
twelve boards. It paid 699.99 dollars, shipping included. That was about a twentieth of the listed price two years
earlier. The channel tried to turn the rack into a cloud gaming server. You can watch it in
[Twelve Node Cloud Gaming Server - AsRock Rack 4U12G BC-250](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BRiVjtwkivE). The
conclusion was that it could not be used for gaming, commercially or otherwise, with the firmware and drivers of
the time.

That changed as the community took the board apart. Its work made the board the low-cost gaming board known today:

- modified firmware
- Linux drivers that support its graphics
- tools for unlocking and tuning it, such as this suite

## What is on it

- **Processor:** AMD BC-250 (*Ariel*), 6 Zen 2 cores at up to about 3.5 GHz.
- **Graphics:** RDNA 2 with 24 compute units enabled (codename *cyan-skillfish*).
- **Memory:** 16 GB GDDR6, shared between the processor and the graphics.
- **Ports:** DisplayPort, Gigabit Ethernet, two USB 3.0 and two USB 2.0, and an M.2 2280 slot for an NVMe or SATA
  SSD.
- **Power:** about 220 W, through an 8-pin 12 V PCIe power connector.

The boards were built for a server case with its own airflow. A board on its own needs a power supply and
fans on the heat sink. The memory runs hot too. Hardware video encoding and decoding do not work. The firmware for
the video engine is missing.
